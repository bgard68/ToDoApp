How TaskBoard keeps your tasks yours

If you’ve ever stayed in a hotel, you already understand how TaskBoard protects your data. Every time you click something, it passes four checkpoints before it touches your tasks.

Checkpoint 3

The door lock

Reads your key card every time.

Checkpoint 4

Your room

Two words people mix up

“Who are you?” is a different question from “Are you allowed?”

Everyday example · The airport

At security, an agent checks your driver’s license: are you who you say you are? At the gate, the agent checks your boarding pass: are you allowed on this plane? Same trip, two different checks.

Checkpoint 2 · Signing in

Check-in happens once. You leave with two things.

You

Type your email and password, like handing over your ID at the hotel desk.

The desk

Checks your password against a scrambled copy on file. TaskBoard never stores the real password, so even its own records can’t reveal it.

You receive
Item 1

A room key card

Used at every door. Unlike a hotel card, it stops working after 15 minutes.

Item 2

A renewal pass in your room safe

Locked away where the web page itself can’t reach it. Good for 7 days.

Why two items? The key card is used constantly, so it expires fast. The renewal pass is used rarely and stays locked up.

What the key card is

Anyone can read it. Nobody can fake it.

When you sign in, TaskBoard gives your browser a digital key card. It lists who you are and when it expires, in plain view.

Everyday example · Your driver’s license

Anyone can read your name and birthday off it. But the hologram makes a fake obvious. Scratch out the birth year and write a new one, and any bouncer can tell.

TaskBoard’s key card has its own hologram, a digital one that only TaskBoard knows how to make. Change one letter on the card, say, to pretend you’re the manager, and the hologram no longer matches.

Checkpoint 3 · Every single click

The door lock runs five checks, every time

Check 1

Is the hologram real?

Made by TaskBoard, and nothing on the card has been changed.

Check 2

Right hotel?

A card from another hotel doesn’t work here.

Check 3

Still in date?

Not past its 15-minute expiry.

Check 4

Still a guest?

A closed account is turned away.

Check 5

Does it fit the current lock?

If the locks were changed, old cards fail. More on this shortly.

All pass

Green light. The door opens.

Any fail

Red light. TaskBoard asks you to sign in again.

Picture a gym that gives you a new day pass each morning. You hand in yesterday’s pass and get today’s. Each pass works once, so a lost pass is only good for a day.

The app takes the renewal pass from the safe and hands it to the desk.

The desk

You

Checkpoint 4 · Who can see what

Your key opens your room, and nobody else’s

Level 1 · The hotel

Must have a key card

Every task and category needs a valid card. Only sign-in, sign-up and renewal are open to the public.

Level 2 · Your room

Only your own things

Every lookup is filtered by the name on your card. You can’t see, change or delete another guest’s tasks.

Level 3 · The manager

Guest or manager

Managers can change the locks on a compromised account. Even a manager can’t walk into your room and read your tasks.

Everyday example · Asking for a room number

Call a hotel and ask which room a guest is in, and they won’t say. They won’t even confirm the person is staying there. TaskBoard does the same: ask for someone else’s task and it just says “not found”.

Revocation · When something goes wrong

Lost your house keys? You change the locks.

Everyday example · Your front door

You don’t chase down every copy of a lost key. You call a locksmith and change the lock. Now every key cut for the old lock stops working: yours, the spare under the mat, and the one a stranger picked up. That’s exactly what TaskBoard does.

Tool 1 · Change the lock

New lock code

Every key card carries your account’s current lock code, and the door checks it every time. Change the code and every card ever issued to you stops working instantly, on every device.

Tool 2 · Take back the spares

Cancel the renewal passes

Like collecting spare keys from the neighbor and the dog walker. No one can get a fresh card. And if a pass you already took back shows up at the door, someone copied it, so everything is locked down.

“Sign out everywhere” does both at once

New lock, and every spare collected. Closing an account has the same instant effect.

Checkpoint 1 · Rate limiting

Too many wrong guesses? You wait.

Everyday example · Your phone’s passcode

Type the wrong passcode a few times and your phone says “try again in 1 minute.” A real owner barely notices. A thief guessing thousands of codes gets nowhere. TaskBoard works the same way.

sign-in tries per minute from one home or office connection.

Everything else
200

clicks per minute from one connection, even after you’re signed in. Far more than a person ever needs.

Over the limit
“Slow down”

TaskBoard says how many seconds to wait, then lets you back in.

The doorman stands before the check-in desk, so a flood is stopped before TaskBoard spends any effort checking passwords. One limitation: people sharing one connection, like an office, share one allowance.
